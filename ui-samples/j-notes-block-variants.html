Client notes — 6 aesthetic variants

Same content, six different visual languages. The current production treatment uses the same uppercase/tracking-wider header pattern as every other section label on the page — so it doesn't read as "this is a free-text memo from a human". These variants bring in paper / handwriting / margin metaphors to differentiate notes from system metadata.

Sample text used in every variant: "Dental practice, 2 dentist shareholders. New K-1 process this year — make sure FYE matches".

N1 · Sticky note (Post-it metaphor)

Most literal — the note looks like a yellow Post-it. Soft amber gradient, faint warm shadow, no border. Strongest visual differentiator from all the other panels on the page (which are white/grey rectangles), instantly readable as "human writing on paper".

N2 · Callout with left bar (Notion / docs feel)

Modern docs / Notion convention: thick colored left bar + soft tinted bg + emoji-or-icon prefix. Reads as "callout, pay attention to this". Less paper-like than N1, more "in-product UI primitive".

N3 · Index card (library / ledger / 50+ CPA persona)

Cream paper bg + double-line top border + ruled horizontal lines + serif body text. Speaks the visual language your CPA persona grew up with — physical client cards in a Rolodex. Most "old-school office" of the bunch.

N4 · Highlighted text (minimal — text is the UI)

No box, no border. The note text itself is highlighted with a faux-marker yellow gradient (CSS box-decoration-break for multi-line). The eyebrow label sits above with a small icon. Most restrained — relies on the highlight to do the visual work.

N5 · Floating pill label (elegant card)

Clean white card with subtle shadow. The "Notes" pill is anchored to the top edge with a negative offset, breaking the rectangle's silhouette so the eye recognises this isn't another stat panel. The most "designer SaaS" of the bunch.

N6 · Notebook paper (legal pad / handwritten)

Faint blue ruled lines + red left margin (the iconic legal-pad pattern). Optionally renders the note in a casual hand-style font for full effect. Strongest "this is informal, written by a person" signal — but the handwritten font might fight legibility for older eyes; keep it sans for production.

My picks:

N1 (sticky note) — strongest at-a-glance "this is a memo, not metadata". Plays well with your dense Salesforce-Lightning chrome elsewhere because it's clearly different.

N2 (callout) — safest if you want to stay in design-system land. Yellow tint + bar bridge to "callout / pay attention" without committing to paper metaphor.

N3 (index card) — best fit for the 50+ CPA persona that grew up on physical Rolodex cards. Most distinctive of all six.

N4 (highlighter) — minimalist's pick. Doesn't add chrome, just treats the text differently.

N5 (floating pill) — most "modern SaaS" — works well if you want consistent typography hierarchy throughout the app.

N6 (notebook) — fun but the lines + margin make it feel less professional than the others. Keep the sans-serif version if you go this way; skip the handwritten font.

If forced to pick one: N1 sticky note. It does the most work with the least chrome — fastest visual signal of "human-written memo" — and the warm amber breaks up the cool-grey desk-app monotony in a way that's actually pleasant rather than gimmicky.
